# Ground Truth: the rescues

*A brainstorm (Fable 5.1, Nov 8 2026). Nothing here is built. Christopher's words: "rescues as the higher-stakes moments." This is ten of them: seven in the story, between the jobs that exist, and three you can do again and again. Each one uses the truck, the Heron and your feet, and each one makes a piece of gear matter. Two of them need the new water landings (a sandbar in the gorge, a beach on the lake).*

Places are given in the brainstorm's kilometres (x east, y north), the way `src/gt-world.js` places everything with `GX(x)`, `GZ(y)`. One brainstorm kilometre is about 1.3 kilometres in the world. The Heron cruises at about 3.5 world kilometres a minute (137 mph); the truck in Sim on a dirt track does about 0.8 a minute, less in sand. The clocks below are set from those numbers with room to spare, so a player who goes straight there makes it and a player who dawdles doesn't.

## The one rule: nobody dies, and the clock never ends the job

The engine's `deliver` step has a `limit`, but when it runs out it **drops the job** ("TOO LATE · THE PAN WRECK", ask again). That's right for a pie delivery and wrong for a rescue. Christopher asked for no game over: too slow should end **worse**, not end.

So every rescue gets a **job clock** (NEW, the same for all ten): a number of minutes on the whole job, shown on the HUD under the job's name as a plain countdown, no red flashing. When it reaches zero nothing stops. The job goes on to its **late ending**: the person is still found and still comes home, but the pay drops (about half), the closing line changes, and in some of them the way home changes too (the beach is under water, so now it's the truck). Some people's later lines remember it. That's the whole system: `clock: 30` on the job, and a second `late:{text, money}` beside the normal ending. Dell's line when it runs out is always the same, dry one: "Clock's gone, pilot. Doesn't change what you do next."

## What's new, in one place (the rescues below refer to these by name)

## 1. Bore Three (story, after The Mail)

**Who and where.** Nkem, at Kettle Creek Station. The man down is **Bowen** (new: a station hand, sun-cracked, a hat older than you are). Bore 3 of the Windmill Line, at about (25.5, 16.0), 9 km of savanna north-east of the station.

**The situation.** Bowen rolled the station's quad bike checking the bores and his leg is broken. He has his radio, so he's talking, but he's lying in the open. **Clock: 40 minutes**, the sun going down; after dark Nkem says "he'll be there in the morning, and it'll be a bad night." (The job sets the hour to late afternoon when it starts.)

**How you find him.** No search: he's at a bore, and he's tied his red shirt to the windmill's vane so you'll know which one. The quad bike is on its side beside it (one model). You see the shirt from 500 m.

**The steps.**
1. `talk` Nkem at Kettle Creek (you fly there from Roadhead: `land{1}`). "Bring the kit." `need: firstaid`.
2. `drive{25.5, 16.0, r 20}` in the truck, up the Windmill Line, past bores 1 and 2. The creek line before bore 3 is soft sand (the truck's first place to bog).
3. `pickup` Bowen, on foot or in the truck, 90 kg. `need: firstaid`.
4. `drive` back to the Kettle Creek strip, stopped beside the Heron. If you roll the truck with him aboard, he's hurt worse.
5. `land{0}` at Roadhead with him, where the nurse meets you on the apron.
6. `talk` Dell.

**Home, and if you're slow.** He flies to Roadhead in the Heron (90 kg: the first time the plane feels a passenger). Late: he comes home the same way, but the job pays $500 instead of $1,100, and Nkem's next job starts "Bowen's leg set badly. Dark does that."

**Pay and heart.** $1,100. Bowen, as you lift him: "Don't tell Nkem I came off at a walking pace." On the apron: "Pilot. That was a soft landing. I noticed."

**To build.** The job clock; the pickup step; the first-aid kit on Teo's wall; one quad-bike-on-its-side model; a shirt on a windmill (a flag card); Bowen's portrait; the job setting the hour.

## 2. The Walker (story, after The Pan Wreck)

**Who and where.** Nkem calls Dell, Dell calls you: "Nobody in the wreck means he walked. He walked east." The Pan Wreck is at (6, 5); the driver is somewhere between the salt and the creek sand east of Pan Strip (9, 7), around (13.5, 8.5), where the pan's edge turns into Ochre Creek's soft bed.

**The situation.** The wreck's driver has been walking for a day with one bottle of water toward the station lights. He's off the salt now and into the creek sand, where the heat shimmers and the truck can bog. **Clock: 30 minutes.** Nkem: "Noon on the pan is forty-five degrees. He's got till about then."

**How you find him.** From the air, low and slow over the pan's east edge: an **SOS of stones** he laid on the salt before he left it (12 m letters, white on white but shadowed), then a **mirror flash** from the creek line when he hears the engine.

**The steps.**
1. `spot{13.5, 8.5, r 400, search 2500}` in the Heron, under 150 m. The map shows a circle east of Pan Strip, not a dot.
2. `land{2}` on the salt at Pan Strip.
3. `drive{13.5, 8.5, r 25}`: 6 km east, salt then creek sand. The compressor helps (tyres down to 15 psi); without it, the winch or a $200 tow.
4. `pickup` on foot, 80 kg, `need: water` (the water can: "Give him the can first. All of it.") and `need: firstaid`.
5. `drive` back to Pan Strip. `land{0}` at Roadhead. `talk` Dell.

**Home, and if you're slow.** The Heron to Roadhead. Late: you find him lying down and not flashing (the mirror stops; the job's spot radius grows to 800 so the SOS alone will do it), he comes home, pays $600 instead of $1,300, and Dell says "Nkem says he'll keep his hands. Just."

**Pay and heart.** $1,300. On the salt, when you reach him: "I kept walking at the lights. They never got closer." In the Heron over the station: "Those lights? That's where I was going? That's it?"

**To build.** The spot step with its search circle; the SOS stones decal; the mirror flash sprite; the water can; the heat clock is only the clock.

## 3. Going Under (story, after The Congregation; a sandbar landing)

**Who and where.** Harker, by radio, from the delta: "Mate. My gorge crew's truck is on a bar in the river and the river's coming up." Flight Service, flat as ever: "Rain on the tableland since dawn. The gorge rises after fourteen hundred." Two Meridian men and their survey truck, axle-deep on a gravel bar in the gorge river at about (41.2, 35.5), 4.7 km upstream of The Bar strip (42, 32).

**The situation.** The river is rising about a metre over the next twenty minutes. The bar their truck is stuck on goes under; so does the **sandbar 300 m downstream that the Heron can land on**. **Clock: 20 minutes** to the sandbar going under. After that the sandbar is gone and there's only the hard way.

**How you find them.** You fly the gorge low from The Bar upstream, walls either side (the brainstorm's fly-through). Their truck's **hazard lights blink** amber on the bar, and one of them waves a jacket (a flag card). No search circle needed: Harker gives you "about five k above the Bar".

**The steps.**
1. `fly{41.2, 35.5, r 300}` in the Heron, up the gorge from The Bar, under 120 m (the step's `below: 120` is a small addition to `fly`).
2. **NEW landing on the sandbar** at (41.3, 35.2): `land{sandbar: 'gorge-1'}`, a short bar right at the surface, downstream of them. The approach is down the gorge between the walls; the Heron must be slow.
3. `foot` to their truck, 300 m along the bar and through knee-deep water (you can walk in water; the truck is the one that bogs).
4. `pickup` two men, 170 kg together, `need: firstaid` (one has a cut head).
5. Back to the Heron on foot; take off downstream with 170 kg aboard (the roll is longer: the sandbar is 350 m and the Heron needs about 250 at this weight).
6. `land{5}` at The Bar, where their second truck is waiting, or `land{8}` at Delta Strip. `talk` Harker (he's at the delta camp).

**The hard way (late).** When the clock runs out the sandbar is under water and the landing step is replaced: `land{5}` at The Bar, then `drive` the gorge bottom 4.7 km upstream, fording the river at two places where it's 1.2 m deep now: **the snorkel** or you don't get there (without it the job tells you plainly: "TOO DEEP WITHOUT A SNORKEL. Land at the Bar and wait for it to drop" and a 10-minute wait does that, the water easing back to 0.7 m). The crew have climbed to a ledge; you pick them up on foot from the gorge track. Their truck is lost to the river (the model is gone when the water drops). Pays $900 instead of $1,800. Harker: "Truck's insured, mate. Men aren't. Thanks."

**Pay and heart.** $1,800, plus $400 from Harker if you also `tow` their truck off the bar before you leave (the tow strap, and only possible before the clock runs out). One of the crew, in the Heron: "I've surveyed this river for three years and never seen it from up here." Haugen, when she hears: "You flew *down* the gorge? Copy. I mean: are you insane? Copy."

**To build.** The sandbar landing (planned); rising water in the canyons region; a Meridian survey truck model with hazard lights; the pickup step; the tow step; the `below` height on `fly`; Flight Service as a text voice.

## 4. Stuck in the Sea (story, after The Count, before Dust)

**Who and where.** Harker: "Two of my blokes drove into the dunes after a seismic line and they're not driving out." Their survey truck is axle-deep between the Caravan's cairns 8 and 9, at about (12.5, 24.7), 6.6 km west of Three Wells along Venn's line.

**The situation.** They left the firm valley floor to climb a slipface, and the truck dug itself in to the doors. They have water till the afternoon and it's 42 degrees. **Clock: 50 minutes.** This is the dune corridor before the Dust job sends you down it at night: the player learns Venn's line in daylight, which is the point.

**How you find them.** `spot` from the air over the dune valleys: when the Heron comes within 2 km they light an **orange smoke** flare; a column leans off the slipface. Before the smoke, the truck is a dust-coloured box in a dune valley you'd miss.

**The steps.**
1. `spot{12.5, 24.7, r 500, search 3000}` in the Heron. The Heron can't land in the dunes (Venn's strip isn't found yet).
2. `land{3}` at Three Wells.
3. `drive{12.5, 24.7, r 40}` west along the cairns, `need: ladders`. The compressor makes the difference between a drive and a fight (15 psi).
4. `pickup` the two men, 170 kg, `need: water`.
5. Optional: `tow` their truck onto the firm valley floor, `need: towstrap` (+$500: "They can drive it out behind you").
6. `drive` back to Three Wells. `land{0}` at Roadhead with both aboard (170 kg: the heaviest the Heron has carried; from a 500 m sand strip, uphill to the west, land uphill and take off downhill, the tundra tyres help), or `land{8}` at the delta.
7. `talk` Harker.

**Home, and if you're slow.** The Heron to Roadhead. Late: one of them has heat stroke; he still comes home, but the job pays $800 instead of $1,600, the tow bonus is gone (they're past caring about the truck), and Harker's next line is quieter: "Fewer jokes today, mate."

**Pay and heart.** $1,600 (+$500 for the truck). One of the men, in the back of the truck, as you pass a cairn: "Who put these here?" You can't answer; Haugen does later: "Venn's line. They used it and never knew."

**To build.** The spot step; the orange smoke; the survey truck model (shared with 3); the tow step; the water can; nothing else new.

## 5. Flamingo Beach (story, after The Count; a beach landing)

**Who and where.** Nkem: "June Pell's out on the lake shore photographing cranes and she's not answering. The river's up." **June Pell** (new: a bird photographer, seventy, a hat with a chin strap, a camera as long as her arm). On the west shore of the lake, a flat beach at about (41.6, 45.0), 4 km of reed and mud from Delta Strip (43, 42) with no track.

**The situation.** The same tableland rain as Going Under has reached the delta a day later. The lake is rising a metre, the levee track is under water, and June's beach, with June on it, goes under in **25 minutes**. She's fine; she's seventy and standing in reeds.

**How you find her.** A **signal fire** on the beach: grey smoke you see from 10 km. The flamingos lift off the shallows as you come over (the herd code already does this).

**The steps.**
1. `fly{41.6, 45.0, r 400}` low over the lake's west shore.
2. **NEW landing on the beach** at (41.6, 45.1): `land{beach: 'lake-west'}`, hard sand 400 m long, water on one side, reeds on the other. Birds on the threshold.
3. `foot` to the fire. `pickup` June, 60 kg plus her camera bag 20 kg.
4. Take off along the beach. `land{8}` at Delta Strip (Harker's camp has a kettle) or `land{1}` at Kettle Creek for Nkem.
5. `talk` Nkem.

**The hard way (late).** The beach goes under. June has walked 500 m inland onto a termite-mound rise. Now it's the truck from Delta Strip across the delta mud and 1.3 m of water in two channels: **the snorkel**, the winch for the mud. You pick her up on foot from the rise and drive her out the same way. Pays $700 instead of $1,400. June: "I got the cranes. Whatever else happened, I got the cranes."

**Pay and heart.** $1,400. June, on the beach, before she'll get in: "One more. The light's perfect." (A ten-second wait, her camera clicking: the job won't let you take off for ten seconds.) In the Heron: "Nkem will say I'm too old for this. Nkem is right. Write that down."

**To build.** The beach landing (planned); rising water in the delta region; the signal-fire smoke (grey; shared with 4's orange); June's portrait; a camera bag as a cargo item is just weight.

## 6. Cinders (story, after Harker's Crate)

**Who and where.** Ruben, slowly, from Last Well: "One of Harker's charge men is down in the caldera. Turned his ankle on the a'a. His mate's on the radio. He can't carry him out." Inside the Caldera, on the lava of the crater floor's north side at about (6.9, 44.6), 700 m across broken a'a from the Caldera strip (6.2, 44).

**The situation.** The man is on ground **no truck can cross** (the lava field: the game already says so) and the Heron can't land any nearer than the strip. So it's your feet. The crater floor goes into shadow at four and it's cold at night on the cinders. **Clock: 30 minutes.** (The job sets the hour to mid-afternoon.)

**How you find him.** From the air, **orange smoke** on the crater floor as you spiral down over the crater lake. On foot, across the lava, you can't see him over the ridges: his mate blows a **whistle** (a sound that gets louder as you close; cheap, and uncanny on the lava).

**The steps.**
1. `land{9}` at the Caldera: the spiral down over the lake, land toward the wall.
2. `foot{6.9, 44.6, r 10}`: 700 m across the a'a on foot. The ground is rough; the walk is slow. `need: firstaid`.
3. `pickup` the hurt man, 85 kg: you walk at 60% now. His mate comes too (second `pickup`, 80 kg).
4. Back to the Heron on foot. Take off from the cinders with 165 kg aboard, climb out of the crater (the rim is 300 m above the strip: a real climb; the climb propeller earns its money here).
5. `land{11}` at Last Well (Ruben has a bed and a radio: "Sit a while. No? This time, yes.") or `land{0}` at Roadhead for the nurse. `talk` Ruben or Dell.

**Home, and if you're slow.** The Heron. Late: dark comes into the crater while you're walking; the lantern is the only light (`need: lantern` appears if the clock runs out); the man is cold through, pays $800 instead of $1,700, and Harker's charge men are "off the job a month".

**Pay and heart.** $1,700. The hurt man, as you lift him: "Don't look at the rocks. Look at the plane." Ruben, after: "The old people walked this. Barefoot. Think about that while your ankles hurt."

**To build.** The pickup step on foot; the whistle sound (one tone, distance-faded); the orange smoke (shared); setting the hour. Walking on a'a already works (the terrain is the floor). Nothing else.

## 7. A Light in the Spring Chamber (story, after The Switchback)

**Who and where.** Haugen, worried for once, from the roadhouse: "Wren hasn't checked in. She went up to the Terraces at noon to draw the Spring Chamber. The cloud's come down." **Wren Okafor** (new: Haugen's field assistant, twenty-four, big glasses, never stops drawing). The Terraces temple at (28.6, 37.8), 1.3 km from Terrace Strip (28, 37), at 1,900 m on the tableland.

**The situation.** Wren went down into the Spring Chamber with a lantern and a sketchbook and turned her ankle on the wet channel floor; the chamber is below the temple and she can't climb out. Night at 1,900 m is near freezing and **the cloud is on the strip**. **Clock: 40 minutes** to full dark and cold. (The job sets the hour to late afternoon and puts the highland cloud on.)

**How you find her.** The strip is in cloud: you find it by the windsock and the waiting truck's lights (the truck is already there, lights on: the brainstorm's reason it's always waiting). In the Terraces, in the dark below, **her lantern**: a warm point of light on the chamber floor, seen from the top of the stair. No person: a light, a sketchbook beside it, her voice on your radio: "Is that you? Don't come down the way I did."

**The steps.**
1. `land{7}` at Terrace Strip in cloud (visibility 300 m; the cloud band from 1,800 to 2,100 m).
2. `drive{28.6, 37.8, r 40}` to the Terraces.
3. `foot` into the temple, down to the top of the Spring Chamber stair. `need: lantern`.
4. **The rope**: `need: rope`. The stair she fell on is wet stone (a blocker the rope lets you pass: the same as the city's tower). Down to her light.
5. `pickup` Wren, 58 kg, `need: firstaid`. Back up and out at 60% speed, the lantern lighting the way.
6. `drive` to the strip. Take off into cloud, climb through it to clear sky and the sunset above (the brainstorm's "one of the game's moments", here with someone aboard who's never seen it).
7. `land{0}` at Roadhead. `talk` Haugen.

**Home, and if you're slow.** The Heron. Late: she's hypothermic; comes home; pays $700 instead of $1,500; Haugen's next three jobs mention "Wren, who is staying in town this week, by my order."

**Pay and heart.** $1,500. Wren, when your lantern finds hers: "I finished the drawing. Before I fell. I want that noted." Above the cloud, sunset: "Oh. *Oh.* Can I draw this? Hold it steady." Haugen, on the apron, not on the radio: "Copy. Thank you. That one I mean."

**To build.** The highland cloud band; the lantern-light signal; a sketchbook find (one small model, and it goes in the field book); Wren's portrait; a rope blocker at the chamber stair (the city's tower has the same). The Spring Chamber itself isn't built: until it is, her light is in the Terraces' lowest terrace.

## 8. The Rim (side, repeatable once the Congregation is done)

**Who and where.** Dell: "Charter Cessna's bent its gear at Rim Strip. Pilot's fine. His passenger has a heart. The strip's blocked." Rim Strip (40, 22), on the mesa with a cliff at each end. The nearest other strip is The Bar (42, 32), 13 km of the Rim Track away, down in the gorge.

**The situation.** A bent light plane across the middle of Rim Strip: you **can't land there** (the `land{4}` step is locked while the wreck's on it; a new strip state). The passenger needs to be off the mesa in **60 minutes**; he's on his tablets but the pilot's frightened. The afternoon crosswind has the windsock straight out.

**How you find them.** You don't need to: the bent plane is across the strip, white against red rock, seen from 5 km. The tension is the road.

**The steps.**
1. `fly{40, 22, r 400}` over Rim Strip to see it's blocked (Dell: "Told you. The Bar, then the Rim Track.")
2. `land{5}` at The Bar in the gorge.
3. `drive{40, 22, r 60}`: 13 km of the Rim Track, ledges and loose rock up to the mesa top (the canyon plateau's steps are built: the track picks a way up them). The winch for one ledge where the truck slides back; without it, the long way round the mesa's south nose adds 6 km.
4. `pickup` two: the passenger, 95 kg, `need: firstaid`; the pilot, 80 kg.
5. `drive` back down to The Bar. Gently: a rollover with the passenger aboard is the worst thing that can happen in this job (hurt worse, pay halves).
6. `land{0}` at Roadhead. `talk` Dell.

**Home, and if you're slow.** The Heron from The Bar, with 175 kg aboard off a 450 m gravel bar: the heaviest short-strip take-off in the game, and the point of the job. Late: the passenger is "grey" when you get him to the nurse; pays $650 instead of $1,400. Repeat: the wreck is gone next time (Teo "went out with a trailer") and the Rim is open again, until the job comes round again with a different bent plane.

**Pay and heart.** $1,400. The charter pilot, in your truck: "I've got six hundred hours. I've got six hundred hours and I did that." The passenger: "Son, I paid him to see the Arches. You want to fly me under them on the way home? No? Good answer."

**To build.** A light plane with bent gear (one model); the strip-blocked state; the pickup step; the tow strap doesn't come into it. The Rim Track as a drawn track would help but the mesa is drivable today.

## 9. The Cold Box (side, repeatable; the first time it's Ruben)

**Who and where.** Dell, fast, no preamble: "Snakebite at Last Well. Ruben. Brown, he says, about so long. The antivenom's in the roadhouse fridge. Go." Last Well (4, 38), 54 world kilometres from Roadhead: the longest haul in the game, at dusk.

**The situation.** Ruben has tied his leg and is sitting still, the way you do. The cold box has to be in his hands in **25 minutes** (the flight is about 15 at full cruise, more with a headwind, less with the big engine). It's dusk when you take off and dark when you get there, and Ruben **can't walk out to put the truck's lights on the strip** the way he usually does: the strip is dark. (The job sets the hour to dusk.) Later repeats: a stockman at Kettle Creek at dawn, a Meridian hand at the delta at midnight (the drill rig's lights show you the way from 20 km).

**How you find him.** The strip is unlit. The windsock, the pale sand against the dark lava, and **one window of Ruben's shack lit** (a warm point light). You line up on the sand by the window light and your own landing light. The light bar on the waiting truck would help if anyone could switch it on; nobody can. (The brainstorm's landing lights for the Heron would be a new upgrade here, $900: "Night strips".)

**The steps.**
1. `talk` Dell on the apron: he hands you the **cold box** (a job item, 15 kg, in the Heron).
2. `land{11}` at Last Well, at night, unlit.
3. `foot` to Ruben's shack with the box: `deliver{x, z, r 6}` on foot. `need: firstaid`.
4. `pickup` Ruben, 90 kg (he insists on walking to the plane: "I've walked further bitten worse.")
5. `land{0}` at Roadhead, the one lit runway. `talk` Dell: the nurse takes him.

**Home, and if you're slow.** The Heron. Late: Ruben is sicker; he lives; a week in Roadhead; pays $700 instead of $1,500; for the next three jobs his column of light at Last Well is dark and his lines come from the roadhouse ("They've got me in a bed with wheels. Me."), and the Fuel to Last Well side job is paused till he's back.

**Pay and heart.** $1,500. Ruben, when you put the box in his hands: "Took your time. Joking. Sit a while. No? All right. Fly." In the Heron, looking down at the dark: "Venn flew at night once. Said the dunes were silver. He was right, you know."

**To build.** The job setting the hour (dusk); the cold box item; the shack's window light; the strip-dark state (no truck lights at the threshold for this job); the pickup step. The landing light upgrade is optional.

## 10. Spot the Stray (side, repeatable from The Mail on)

**Who and where.** Dell or Nkem, whoever heard the call. The first time it's **the Lindqvists** (a couple in a hire SUV, "following a map they printed"), bogged in Ochre Creek's sand at (26.5, 13.5). After that the stray is one of six places, picked at random each time: the creek crossing on the Station Track; the Pan's north edge; the Causeway's first creek ford; the Marbles' back track; the delta levee's mud; the Switchback's second hairpin.

**The situation.** Hire car, two people, no idea. They have water and they're embarrassed, not dying, but the sun is the sun. **Clock: 45 minutes** by day. At dusk (one of the six places picks night half the time) the clock is the dark: 30 minutes, and then they **fire a flare** once a minute.

**How you find them.** `spot` from the air: by day, the **dust plume** from their spinning wheels, then the car itself (one hire-SUV model in white, silver or red); at night, the **flare**, red, rising and drifting, once a minute.

**The steps.**
1. `spot{x, z, r 400, search 2000}` in the Heron. The map shows the circle Dell gave you ("Somewhere on the Station Track. 'Near a tree,' they said.")
2. `land` at the nearest strip (the job picks it: Kettle Creek, Pan Strip, Rim, Delta or Switchback).
3. `drive` to them.
4. `tow` them out, `need: towstrap`: TOW, five seconds, the hire car slides back onto the track and its hazards stop blinking. Or, if it's night or they're done with the country: `pickup` the two of them (160 kg) and drive them to the strip; the car stays for Teo.
5. `land{0}` at Roadhead. `talk` Dell.

**Home, and if you're slow.** Towed out, they drive themselves home behind you (their car is simply gone next time you pass). Picked up, they fly with you. Late: a flare in the dark instead of a dust plume in the day, and $350 instead of $700; Dell: "Charge them double. I'm joking. Half."

**Pay and heart.** $700 by day, $900 at night. Mrs Lindqvist, as the car comes out of the sand: "We were told the road ends at Roadhead." Dell, later: "It does."

**To build.** The spot step (shared); the hire SUV model with hazard lights and a dust plume (the truck's own dust effect, parked); the flare; the tow step and the tow strap; six stuck places as a list.

## The four to build first

The best mix of excitement and least new building, in the order I'd build them, because each one adds one thing to the last:

1. **Bore Three.** The gentlest setting for the two things every rescue needs: the job clock and a passenger. One new model (the quad on its side), one new gear item (the first-aid kit), and the first time the Heron feels someone aboard. If the clock and the pickup feel right here, they'll feel right everywhere.

2. **The Walker.** Adds the spot step and two signals (the SOS stones and the mirror flash), on ground that's already built and already bogs. It follows straight on from The Pan Wreck, which already ends "then he walked", so it closes a story the game has already started.

3. **Going Under.** The exciting one: down the gorge between the walls, a sandbar landing the next build already brings, two men aboard off a short bar, and a late ending that turns a flying job into a snorkel drive through the same river. Adds the survey truck model, rising water and the tow step; the tow step and the truck model are then free for Stuck in the Sea.

4. **The Cold Box.** Night, the longest flight in the game, an unlit strip found by one window, and Ruben, who people like. Adds only the hour-setting, the cold box and one point light, and then it repeats forever with a different patient. It's also the natural place for the Heron's landing lights as an upgrade.

After those four, Stuck in the Sea comes almost free (spot, smoke, the truck model and the tow all exist), and Flamingo Beach needs only the beach landing and the grey smoke. A Light in the Spring Chamber is the one to save for when the highland cloud is built; it's the best-written of the ten and deserves the cloud.
